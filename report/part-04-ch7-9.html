Chapter 7

Screen Analysis — Load Monitoring

The complete working list of every monitored location, and the drill-down an engineer uses to investigate one of them.

7.1 Purpose

To expose every individual monitored location with its live consumption, its limits, its trend and its status — searchable, filterable and sortable.

7.2 Why this screen exists

The Overview establishes that something is wrong; this screen establishes exactly what and where. It is the working surface for anyone investigating a specific asset, and the only screen presenting all 110 register entries at once.INTERP

7.3 Sections

7.3.1 Load group cards

Purpose
Give a per-group summary and act as a one-click filter for the table below.
Data used
Group roll-ups; worst severity among the group's points; count of points offline.IMPL
Behaviour
Clicking a card filters the table to that group and scrolls to it. Clicking again clears the filter without scrolling.IMPL
Interpretation
Severity and communications state are shown side by side, never one instead of the other — a group holding a critical alarm must not read as merely "offline" because one of its meters is also unreachable.

7.3.2 Filter row

Filters are covered in full in Chapter 15. In summary: all four combine with AND logic, and the result count updates live.IMPL

7.3.3 The point table

ColumnUnitMeaning and how it is derived
Tag—The point identifier, keyed to the register. Monospaced for scanning.
Location—Asset name, with physical location and unit count beneath.
NowkWThe smoothed current reading. Shows an em-dash if the meter is offline or the point is unmonitored.
TypicalkWExpected demand for this hour: rated demand × the group's diurnal profile factor.

Table 7.1 — Point table columns.IMPL

Sorting
Any column heading sorts. The default is severity descending, so problems rise to the top without interaction. Re-sorting is suppressed while the pointer or keyboard focus is inside the table, so rows do not move under the reader.IMPL
What to look for
High % of limit; large positive vs typical; any non-Normal status; an Updated value that has stopped advancing.

7.3.4 Point detail panel

Purpose
Everything known about one location, opened by clicking a row or pressing Enter on it.
Contents
A trend chart with warning, alarm and rated levels drawn as labelled reference lines; fifteen figures including smoothed and unsmoothed values, typical, deviation, rated demand, both limits, percentage of limit, session peak and time, session energy, unit count and unit rating, group, feeding substation, service state and sample count; and that point's own alarm episode history.IMPL
Interpretation
Seeing the limits drawn on the same axes as the trace answers "how close is this to alarming?" without arithmetic.
Accessibility
Opens with focus on the close control; Escape closes.

7.4 How it works

All 110 rows are built once at start-up and thereafter updated in place; filtering toggles row visibility rather than rebuilding the table. Sorting reorders existing nodes via a document fragment. This keeps a 110-row table updating once per second without flicker.IMPL

7.5 Practical use case

Following the Overview alarm, an engineer opens this screen, types "tower" into search, and finds ADM-TWR-C6C7 at 267 kW against a typical of 216 kW — 24% above normal, 109% of its warning limit. Opening the row shows the trend crossing the warning line about eight minutes ago and remaining above it. The alarm history shows no prior episode for this point, indicating a new condition rather than a recurring one.

Chapter 8

Screen Analysis — Voltage & Frequency

Supply quality: whether the electricity arriving is good, as distinct from how much of it there is.

8.1 Purpose

To present the parameters that describe the quality of supply — frequency, voltage, phase balance and current — against their statutory limits.

8.2 Why this screen exists

Poor power quality damages equipment and wastes money without ever causing an outage. Voltage outside band shortens insulation life and overheats motors; sustained phase imbalance destroys three-phase motors. These conditions are invisible on any consumption view, so they require their own screen.DOMAIN

8.3 Sections

8.3.1 Stat tiles

Worst phase imbalance
Given its own headline because it is the parameter that causes the most expensive damage. Calculated as the maximum deviation of any phase from the three-phase mean, expressed as a percentage of that mean, taken across all three substations. The 2% threshold is a standard guideline.IMPLDOMAIN

8.3.2 System frequency chart

Unit
Hz, two decimal places.
Interpretation
Frequency is a national quantity, not a site one. It reflects whether generation matches demand across the grid. A site cannot influence it; it can only respond to it. Sustained excursion beyond the band is a grid event, not a site fault.DOMAIN

8.3.3 Per-substation voltage charts

Purpose
Localise a voltage problem to a specific substation rather than only knowing the site average.
Interpretation
Three traces moving together indicate a balanced supply. Traces separating indicate imbalance at that substation specifically.

8.4 Practical use case

A maintenance supervisor reports repeated motor failures in one zone. This screen shows the substation feeding that zone running at 2.4% imbalance while the other two sit near 0.9%. That points to unequal single-phase loading on that substation — a wiring distribution problem rather than a motor-quality problem, and one that will keep destroying motors until the loading is rebalanced.

Chapter 9

Screen Analysis — Energy Analytics

The shift from power to energy — from what is happening now to what has accumulated. This is the screen for cost conversations.

9.1 Purpose

To show how much energy has been consumed, how it divides between import, generation and losses, how peaky the demand profile is, and which locations dominate consumption.

9.2 Why this screen exists

Electricity is billed on energy, and frequently on peak demand as well. Every other screen deals in instantaneous power; this one deals in the quantities that appear on an invoice and in a reduction target.DOMAIN

9.3 Sections

9.3.1 Stat tiles

Average demand
Energy consumed ÷ elapsed hours — the mean power over the period, not a mean of samples.IMPL
Load factor
(Average demand ÷ peak demand) × 100. Stated on the tile as "average ÷ peak".IMPL

9.3.2 Energy mix

Calculation
Grid import = consumed − solar generated; the three segments are import, solar and losses.IMPL
Interpretation
The losses segment is energy paid for but never used. Solar directly displaces import.

9.3.3 Load-duration curve

Purpose
Answer a question an ordinary trend cannot: what proportion of the period is spent near peak?
X-axis
Percentage of the period, 0% to 100% — not time of day.
Y-axis
Demand in kW.
Interpretation
A steep drop at the left means peaks are brief and rare, so peak-shaving would yield large savings for little disruption. A flat curve means demand is steady and the peak charge is unavoidable without reducing base load.DOMAIN
Why this chart type
It is the standard instrument for peak-demand analysis in energy management, and it is the only chart on the dashboard where the x-axis is not time.

9.3.4 Energy by group

9.3.5 Top consumers

Purpose
Identify where consumption-reduction effort produces the largest return.
Calculation
Share = point energy ÷ site energy × 100. Generation points are excluded.IMPL
Interpretation
Expect large aggregate tags (MOQ Flats, B Type Qtrs) to dominate, because each represents hundreds of dwellings. A single-unit tag appearing high in this list is the more interesting signal.

9.4 Practical use case

A facilities manager is asked to cut electricity cost by 10%. The load-duration curve shows a steep left-hand drop — demand is near peak for only a small share of the period — so peak-shaving is worth pursuing. Top consumers shows the two largest residential tags accounting for roughly a third of site energy, so tariff or behavioural measures there will move the total more than any plant-side intervention.
