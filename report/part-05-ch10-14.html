Chapter 10

Screen Analysis — Solar

The only screen concerned with electricity being produced rather than consumed.

10.1 Purpose

To report generation from the 13 solar systems and, critically, to judge that output against what the current sun angle should produce rather than against a fixed target.

10.2 Why this screen exists

Solar output varies legitimately by an order of magnitude through the day. A raw output figure therefore cannot distinguish a healthy array at dawn from a failed array at noon. Only a ratio against expected output can, which is why this screen is built around performance ratio rather than kW.DOMAIN

10.3 Sections

10.3.1 Stat tiles

Performance ratio
Output ÷ expected × 100, computed only when expected exceeds 0.5 kW; below that the ratio is meaningless and an em-dash is shown.IMPL
CO₂ avoided
Session solar energy × 0.42 kg/kWh, reported in tonnes.IMPL

10.3.2 Fleet output gauge

The gauge should normally read well below full scale — full scale is peak capacity in ideal sun. The meaningful comparison is between the fill and the tick mark, not between the fill and the end of the arc.

10.3.3 Generation trend

Implementation note

The trend is derived by scaling one array's history by the number of arrays, rather than by summing all thirteen histories. The shape is representative and the magnitude is correct in aggregate, but it will not reflect divergence between individual arrays. Per-array divergence is visible in the table beneath instead.IMPL

Under-output limits are evaluated only during daylight, defined as solar profile factor above 0.2. Outside that window the limits are suspended entirely, so no array alarms overnight for the crime of darkness.IMPL

10.4 Practical use case

At midday the fleet reads 41 kW against 58 kW expected — a 71% performance ratio. The array table shows twelve arrays near 95% and SOL-07 at 12%, flagged Under-output. The problem is one array, not the fleet: shading, soiling or an inverter fault at that location.

Chapter 11

Screen Analysis — Transformers

The only screen containing no simulated values whatsoever.

11.1 Purpose

To document the transformer fleet: how many units of each rating exist, and how installed capacity distributes across the substations.

11.2 Why this screen exists

Transformer capacity is the ceiling on everything else. Every loading percentage elsewhere on the dashboard is computed against these figures, so they warrant their own screen. It is also the screen to use when every displayed number must be defensible — nothing here is modelled.IMPL

Chapter 12

Screen Analysis — Switchgear

The protective equipment between supply and load.

12.1 Purpose

To show the state of the circuit breakers protecting the network, and to summarise the distribution-board population.

12.2 Why this screen exists

Breaker position is the first thing an engineer checks when a section of a site loses supply. A tripped breaker is both the symptom and the location of a fault.DOMAIN

Provenance of this section

The register states "VCBs HT 11 KV — 8 Nos — (TS Rawat, PNS Hafeez, ARC & OM)". The count and the four site names are therefore real. Splitting them into incomer/bus-section pairs is an assumption, because the document names the sites without stating what each breaker does. Positions are simulated entirely; the register records no breaker state.IMPL

12.3.3 Distribution boards

The boards are deliberately summarised rather than listed. The register gives a total of 475 with no names, tags or locations; generating 475 individual board entries would imply a level of detail the source document does not contain. The apportionment shown is by installed kVA and each row says so.IMPL

12.4 Practical use case

A zone reports loss of supply. This screen shows VCB-03 (ARC incomer) reading Tripped with one operation this session. The fault is localised immediately to the ARC incomer, and the operation count distinguishes a single event from repeated tripping.

Chapter 13

Screen Analysis — Alarms & Events

Everything currently wrong, and a permanent record of everything that has been wrong.

13.1 Purpose

To hold the complete alarm state and history, with the timestamps needed to answer post-incident questions.

13.2 Why this screen exists

The Overview shows active alarms but is space-constrained and holds no history. After any incident the question asked is not "what is wrong now?" but "when did we know, and how quickly did we respond?" — which requires a durable record.INTERP

The log retains up to 500 closed episodes in memory. It is not persisted: reloading the page clears it. Export to CSV from the Reports screen before reloading if a record is required.IMPL

13.4 Practical use case

A monthly review asks how many limit exceedances occurred and how promptly they were handled. The log gives episode count by type and severity, and the gap between Raised and Acknowledged gives the response time for each.

14.2 Forecasting

14.2.1 Purpose and rationale

To project demand over the coming 24 hours so peaks can be anticipated and capacity headroom assessed in advance.

14.2.2 Stat tiles

Headroom = (firm capacity − forecast peak) ÷ firm capacity × 100, where firm capacity is the sum of the three substations' usable kW.IMPL

14.2.3 Forecast chart

X-axis
Clock hour, from two hours before now to 22 hours ahead.
Y-axis
Demand in kW, with rated demand marked as a reference.
Band
±6% at the current hour, widening linearly to ±20% at the far horizon.IMPL
Method — stated on the screen itself

This is a profile-based projection, not a trained prediction. Each asset group's rated demand is shaped by its own diurnal curve and the results summed. No historical consumption exists in the register to train a statistical model on. Once several weeks of real telemetry have been recorded, this should be replaced with a model fitted to that history. The dashboard prints this caveat directly beneath the chart.IMPL

14.3 Settings

This screen carries no controls. It exists to hold the provenance statement and the register gap list in a permanent location — the screen to open if a figure is challenged. Display preferences (theme, density, source) live in the header rather than here.IMPL

14.3.1 Practical use case

During a review a stakeholder questions the 16.7 MW connected-capacity figure. Settings shows it derives from 18,530 kVA of installed transformer capacity stated in the register, converted at an assumed 0.9 power factor — and that the per-substation split is an assumption while the total is not.
