18.3 Power versus energy — the central distinction

The distinction in one line

Power is the rate at which electricity is being used, measured at an instant. Energy is the quantity used over a period. Power is the speedometer; energy is the odometer.

18.3.1 The relationship

Energy = Power × Time Worked example (illustrative): A pump draws 10 kW and runs for 5 hours. Energy = 10 kW × 5 h = 50 kWh As actually implemented, per ingest tick: E += P × Δt ÷ 3,600,000 Δt in milliseconds Same example over one 2-second tick at 4,500 kW: E += 4,500 × 2,000 ÷ 3,600,000 = 2.5 kWh

The 3,600,000 divisor converts milliseconds to hours (1 h = 3,600,000 ms). The implementation rejects intervals longer than 60 seconds, so a communications gap does not create a fictitious energy spike.IMPL

19.2.1 Smoothing

src/store.js α = 1 − e^(−Δt / τ) τ = 6,000 ms value = previous + α × (new − previous) Input: raw kW reading, elapsed ms since the last sample Output: smoothed kW | Why: raw jitter reads as plant instability Note: both the smoothed and raw values are retained and displayed

19.2.2 Energy accumulation

src/store.js (per point and site), src/electrical.js (site) E += P × Δt ÷ 3,600,000 gaps > 60,000 ms rejected Input: smoothed kW, elapsed ms Output: kWh

19.2.3 Three-phase electrical derivations

src/electrical.js — all derived from real power P Q = P × tan(arccos(PF)) reactive power, kVAr S = √(P² + Q²) apparent power, kVA PF = P ÷ S site power factor I = (P × 1000) ÷ (√3 × V × 1000 × PF) line current, A These are standard three-phase relationships, so the derived quantities remain mutually consistent by construction.

19.2.4 Modelled quantities (assumptions, not measurements)

src/electrical.js loading = clamp(kW ÷ capacityKw, 0, 1.2) PF_bus = clamp(0.975 − loading×0.09 + 0.006·sin(φ), 0.82, 0.99) V_phase = 11 × (1 − loading×0.028 + imbalance + noise) frequency = clamp(50 + 0.045·sin(φ) + noise, 49.7, 50.3) lossFrac = 0.018 + 0.012 × clamp(kW ÷ 8000, 0, 1) 1.8% .. 3.0% efficiency= (1 − lossFrac) × 100 CO2 = solar kWh × 0.42 kg/kWh

Power factor degrading as load rises, and voltage drooping under load, are physically correct behaviours; the specific coefficients are modelling choices, not measured characteristics.INTERP

19.2.5 Register-derived quantities

src/register.js ratedKw = unit count × estimated unit kW unit kW is ESTIMATED alarmKw = ratedKw × group headroom 1.15 .. 1.40 warnKw = ratedKw + (alarmKw − ratedKw) × 0.55 capacityKw = round(installed kVA × 0.9) 0.9 PF assumed Solar under-output limits are RELATIVE, not absolute: lowAlarm = expected output × 0.35 lowWarn = expected output × 0.60 evaluated only when the solar profile factor exceeds 0.2 (daylight)
Why the warning limit is derived from the gap

An earlier revision set the warning limit at a flat 85% of the alarm limit. For groups with tight headroom — street lighting at 1.15 — that produced a warning limit below rated demand, so those circuits would have alarmed at normal load. Deriving it from the gap between rated and alarm makes that impossible for any headroom value. The verification script asserts the resulting ladder is coherent for every point.IMPL

19.2.6 Analysis calculations

src/store.js, src/pages.js expected = ratedKw × profileFactor(group profile, hour) deltaVsExpected = (value ÷ expected) − 1 pctOfLimit = (value ÷ limit) × 100 avgDemand = energy kWh ÷ elapsed hours loadFactor = (avgDemand ÷ peakDemand) × 100 imbalance = max|V_phase − V_avg| ÷ V_avg × 100 performanceRatio = (output ÷ expected) × 100 if expected > 0.5 kW headroom = (capacity − forecastPeak) ÷ capacity × 100 Forecast, per hour h over a 24-hour horizon: v(h) = Σprofiles ratedByProfile × profileFactor(profile, h) spread = 0.06 + max(0, k−2)/22 × 0.14 band = v × (1 ± spread)

19.2.7 Alarm evaluation

src/limits.js — two independent stages STAGE 1 confirm the physical CONDITION (drives the display) rise: severity confirmed after 6,000 ms continuously exceeded fall: clear requires value < limit × (1 − 0.04) for 12,000 ms (for low limits: value > limit × 1.04) STAGE 2 reconcile ANNUNCIATION (drives the alarm list) open an episode unless within the 90,000 ms re-arm window escalate an existing episode rather than opening a second close only when the condition returns to normal QUALITY stale > 15,000 ms | offline > 40,000 ms start-up grace: never-heard-from is "pending", not an alarm

Separating condition from annunciation is what allows a point to display as over limit while its alarm is held by the re-arm window — anti-flood machinery must never hide a genuine fault.IMPL

19.3.2 Domain relationships not implemented

The following are standard in this field but are absent from this implementation. They are listed so their absence is explicit rather than assumed:

  • Energy → Cost. No tariff data exists and no monetary value is calculated anywhere.
  • Consumption → CO₂. Only avoided CO₂ from solar is computed; the emissions of imported energy are not.

20.3 Data-quality handling

The most important design decision in this area is that quality is a separate axis from severity. A meter that has stopped reporting is not the same fact as a meter correctly reporting zero, and the two are never conflated. The payload contract requires unreachable meters to be omitted rather than zeroed, and the dashboard surfaces them as a distinct communications condition.IMPL

Feed health is watchdogged independently of message content: live → degraded after 8 s of silence → down after 25 s. When the feed dies a banner states so and the view dims, rather than the display freezing on stale values while appearing healthy.

Chapter 22

Technical Findings, Glossary & Conclusion

Observations arising from the analysis, the consolidated glossary, and closing assessment.

22.1 Technical findings

F-1  Gross versus net demand inconsistency

The Total Active Power KPI displays gross site load, while the load trend chart directly beneath it plots net demand (load minus solar generation) and is labelled "Net demand". At present solar contributes under 1% of site demand, so the two differ by a negligible margin, but the labels describe different quantities. If generation capacity grows the divergence becomes material.IMPL Recommendation: align the two, or label the KPI explicitly as gross.

F-2  Solar trend is extrapolated from a single array

The generation trend on the Solar screen scales one array's history by the array count rather than summing all thirteen. Aggregate magnitude is correct and the shape is representative, but divergence between arrays will not appear in that chart. Per-array divergence is visible in the table beneath, so the information is available — but the chart alone would not reveal a single failing array.IMPL

F-3  No persistence

All readings, energy totals and alarm history exist only in browser memory. Reloading the page discards them. For a production deployment a historian would be required, both for record-keeping and to enable the trained forecast that the Forecasting screen currently cannot provide.IMPL

F-4  No authentication or authorisation

Any user reaching the URL has full access, including alarm acknowledgement. Acknowledgement is recorded against the fixed label "operator" rather than an identified user, so the audit trail records that an alarm was acknowledged but not by whom.IMPL

F-5  Every limit rests on estimated ratings

The most consequential finding. No unit kW rating is measured; all are engineering estimates, and every warning and alarm limit derives from them. The alarm logic is sound, but the thresholds it enforces are provisional until real nameplate or metered data replaces the estimates.IMPL

F-6  Documentary discrepancy in the register

The register states 14 tube wells with 12 operational, but lists 11 with locations. One operational tube well is unaccounted for. Raised with the facilities team rather than reconciled.IMPL

F-7  Items that could not be verified

  • Actual per-substation transformer allocation — not stated in the register; could not be verified from the available implementation or source document.
  • Actual feeder-to-load allocation — not stated; could not be verified.
  • Satellite station ratings — the register gives a count of 13 with no kVA; could not be verified.
  • Real breaker roles and positions — four sites are named but roles are not; could not be verified.
  • Distribution board identities — 475 stated as a total with no tags or locations; could not be verified.
  • Any historical consumption — none exists in the source document; could not be verified.

22.3 Conclusion

Power Monitor is a complete and technically sound monitoring application. Its twelve screens cover situational awareness, per-location detail, supply quality, network structure, energy analysis, generation performance, asset inventory, exception handling, reporting and forecasting. The alarm engine follows recognised industrial practice, with on-delay, hysteresis, latching, acknowledgement, ringback and anti-flood re-arm implemented and covered by automated tests.

Its architecture is deliberately minimal — no framework, no charting library, no database, no runtime dependencies — which makes it deployable by copying a folder onto an isolated network, and gives it a single well-defined integration seam at src/source.js.

Its principal limitation is not one of engineering but of input data. The source register is an asset inventory containing no measurements, so every live value is simulated and every alarm threshold rests on an estimated rating. The implementation handles this honestly: provenance is marked at the point of use, documentary discrepancies are displayed rather than hidden, and the forecast discloses its own method. That candour is the report's basis for judging the system ready for connection to real telemetry.

To move from prototype to operational service, three things are required: real metering data in place of the simulated feed; real nameplate ratings in place of the estimated ones, so the limits become defensible; and a historian, without which no record survives a page reload and no trained forecast is possible.

Summary judgement

The application is production-quality in construction and prototype-quality in data. The gap between the two is well documented within the system itself, and closing it is a data-acquisition task rather than a software one.
